# RULES_SPEC.md

## Dice Tests

### Dice Pool

A test rolls a number of d10 equal to the final dice pool after all modifiers.

`final_dice_pool = base_dice_pool + bonuses - penalties`

The minimum final dice pool is 0. If penalties reduce the final dice pool to 0, no dice are rolled and the Test automatically fails.

### Difficulty

Difficulty ranges from 3 to 9.

Default difficulty is 6.

### Successes

Each die showing a value equal to or greater than difficulty produces one raw success.

### Resistance

Resistance is subtracted from raw successes.

Default resistance is 0.

### Special Dice Numbers

Each natural `1` increases resistance by 1.

Each natural `10` produces one success and causes one additional d10 to be rolled.

Additional dice generated by a `10` may themselves explode on another `10`.

### Test Result

After all dice, explosions, modifiers, and resistance have been resolved:

`net_successes = max(0, raw_successes - resistance)`

Negative net successes are never retained.

### Botch

A botch occurs when:

`number_of_rolled_1s > raw_successes`

Botch is determined before net successes are clamped to 0.

### Test Failed

A test fails if:

`net_successes == 0`

The margin of failure is the amount by which resistance exceeded raw successes before clamping:

`margin_of_failure = max(0, resistance - raw_successes)`

### Test Succeeded

A test succeeds when:

`net_successes >= 1`

All successes above 1 are called additional successes:

`additional_successes = max(0, net_successes - 1)`

### Specialization

When an applicable specialization is enabled, test difficulty is reduced by 1 before minimum/maximum difficulty handling.

## Willpower

### Maximum Willpower

`max_willpower = Composure + Resolve`

### Current Willpower

Willpower is represented by a wound track with a number of boxes equal to `max_willpower`.

Willpower boxes can contain:

* empty
* light wound `/`
* heavy wound `X`

### Spending Willpower

Spending one point of Willpower marks one light Willpower wound. 

Character can spend one point of Willpower in the Test to prevent botch and grant 1 automatic success.

### Willpower Wounds

Light Willpower damage marks `/`.

If no empty Willpower boxes remain, each additional light Willpower wound upgrades one existing light wound:

`/ -> X`

Heavy Willpower wounds cannot be upgraded further.

### Exhausted Willpower

If every Willpower box contains at least a light wound, the character receives:

`-2 dice to all Tests`

This penalty remains until at least one Willpower box becomes empty.

### Current Willpower Roll

Current Willpower is equal to the number of completely empty Willpower boxes:

`current_willpower = max_willpower - light_wounds - heavy_wounds`

A Current Willpower Test rolls:

`current_willpower` dice.

### Full Willpower Roll

Full Willpower ignores light Willpower wounds but not heavy wounds:

`full_willpower = max_willpower - heavy_wounds`

A Full Willpower Test rolls:

`full_willpower` dice.

# Health

## Health Points

Maximum Health is:

`max_health = 3 + Strength + Endurance + health_bonus`

The Health track contains exactly `max_health` boxes.

### Health Levels

Health boxes are distributed among five Health Levels:

1. Bruised
2. Hurt
3. Wounded
4. Mauled
5. Crippled

Health boxes are distributed as evenly as possible.

If equal distribution is impossible, remaining boxes are added one at a time starting from the most severe Health Level:

`Crippled -> Mauled -> Wounded -> Hurt -> Bruised`

Example:

`max_health = 7`

Distribution:

* Bruised: 1
* Hurt: 1
* Wounded: 1
* Mauled: 2
* Crippled: 2

### Health Level Penalties

Health Level penalties are:

| Health Level | Penalty |
| ------------ | ------: |
| Bruised      |       0 |
| Hurt         |  -1 die |
| Wounded      | -2 dice |
| Mauled       | -3 dice |
| Crippled     | -5 dice |

The active wound penalty is determined by the most severe Health Level containing at least one heavy or aggravated wound.

Penalties from different Health Levels do not stack.

---

## Wounds

There are three wound severities:

* light `/`
* heavy `X`
* aggravated `Ж`

Health wounds fill the Health track from the least severe Health Level toward the most severe:

`Bruised -> Hurt -> Wounded -> Mauled -> Crippled`

Each Health box can contain only one final wound marker.

### Wound Severity

Wound severity follows this order:

`light < heavy < aggravated`

Two wounds of the same severity combine into one wound of the next severity:

`2 light -> 1 heavy`

`2 heavy -> 1 aggravated`

Aggravated wounds cannot be upgraded further.

### Applying Wounds

When a wound is applied, it occupies the first available Health box.

If the target box already contains a wound of lower severity, the new wound replaces it and the displaced lower-severity wound is pushed forward into the next valid Health box.

If two wounds of the same severity overlap in the same box, they combine into one wound of the next severity.

This process repeats recursively until all wound changes are resolved.

The implementation must resolve the entire cascade automatically.

### Example: Wound Displacement

A character has 7 Health boxes and all 7 currently contain light wounds.

The character receives 2 heavy wounds.

Each new heavy wound replaces one light wound.

The displaced light wounds move forward and combine with existing light wounds.

Final result:

* 4 heavy wounds
* 3 light wounds

# Damage

## Damage Test

Physical damage is normally generated by a successful attack.

Every damaging attack has a `damage_pool`.

A Damage Test rolls:

`damage_pool` d10

Base Damage Test difficulty is:

`6`

Each success on the Damage Test produces:

`1 damage`

Damage is converted into wounds only after armor and other damage modifiers are resolved.

### Additional Attack Successes

Each additional success on the Attack Test reduces Damage Test difficulty by 1.

`damage_difficulty = 6 - additional_attack_successes`

Normal Difficulty minimum/maximum rules still apply.

---

## Damage Type

Every damage source has two independent properties:

1. damage nature;
2. lethality.

### Damage Nature

Damage nature describes how the damage is physically or supernaturally produced.

Examples include:

* impact
* cutting
* piercing
* fire
* electricity
* poison
* supernatural

Damage nature is used to determine whether armor or another defense applies.

### Lethality

Lethality determines the severity of wounds caused by the damage.

#### Subdual Damage

Subdual damage produces light wounds `/`.

#### Normal Damage

Normal damage produces heavy wounds `X`.

If lethality is not explicitly specified, damage is Normal.

#### Deadly Damage

Deadly damage produces aggravated wounds `Ж`.

---

# Concentrated and Distributed Damage

Every damaging source is either:

* concentrated;
* distributed.

### Distributed Damage

Distributed damage applies wounds normally and may occupy any number of Health boxes.

### Concentrated Damage

Concentrated damage represents damage focused on a limited body area.

A single Concentrated Damage instance may produce no more than:

`3 wounds`

Any damage beyond this limit increases wound severity inside those three wounds limit.

Damage must be distributed as evenly as possible between the three wounds.

### Concentrated Damage Examples

Five heavy damage produces:

* 2 aggravated wounds
* 1 heavy wound

Eight light damage produces:

* 2 aggravated wounds
* 1 heavy wound

# Weapons

Weapons define how attacks are performed and how successful attacks generate Damage Tests.

### Weapon Type

Examples:

* unarmed
* melee
* thrown
* bow
* firearm
* other ranged

### Damage Pool

`damage_pool` is the base number of dice used for the Damage Test after a successful attack.

### Damage Nature

The weapon specifies one or more damage natures.

Examples:

* impact
* cutting
* piercing
* fire

### Lethality

Weapon lethality is:

* subdual;
* normal;
* deadly.

Default:

`normal`

### Armor Piercing

Some weapons have:

`armor_piercing = X`

Armor Piercing reduces the effective Protection of applicable armor.

`effective_protection = max(0, armor.protection - armor_piercing)`

Armor Piercing affects both armor absorption and armor degradation.

Armor Piercing does not directly reduce the armor's permanent Protection value before damage resolution.
